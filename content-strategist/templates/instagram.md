# Instagram Templates — "Pranjal the curious creative"

> Warm, intimate, playful, feeling-forward, sensory. Life *and* insight —
> "always through a lens, never random vlogs." Curiosity-first.
> Same engine (Notice → Connect → Reframe), but expressed as *feeling*, not analysis.
> The promise still holds: one new thought, delivered like a friend noticing out loud.

---

## The 4 Instagram formats

### Format A — The Carousel (your strength)
Save-able, swipe-able. One idea, one slide at a time. 6–10 slides.

**Slide architecture:**
```
Slide 1 (COVER):   The hook. A curiosity gap or a reframe-in-progress. Big, few words.
                   Must make someone stop AND want to swipe. Not the whole idea.
Slide 2 (NOTICE):  The specific, real observation. Set the scene, sensory.
Slide 3–5 (CONNECT): One thought per slide. Let the idea travel. White space is your friend.
Slide 6–8 (REFRAME): The turn → the one new thought, given room to land.
Final slide (SOFT CLOSE): A warm line + a gentle invitation to save/share/reply.
                          Optional: "save this for the next time you [X]."
```
Design note: consistent, calm, aesthetic — matches the "one brain" upgrade. Beginner-friendly art welcome. Legible > clever typography.

### Format B — The Caption-led single (photo/painting + words)
A "through a lens" image + a caption that does the engine in miniature. Pillar 4 home turf.

```
[IMAGE]  Your photo / painting / coffee / tiny moment — genuinely beautiful, genuinely yours.
[LINE 1] The noticing, as a whisper. ("I've made my matcha the same way for 300 days.")
[BODY]   3–6 short lines connecting the small thing to a bigger feeling/idea.
[CLOSE]  The reframe as a soft landing, or an open question.
```

### Format C — The Talking-head Reel (you're comfortable on camera)
15–60 sec. One thought, said like you're telling a friend across a café table.
**Script structure — 4 beats** (retention mechanics + the engine):

```
1. HOOK (0–3s)     Scroll-stopping, straight in — no "hi guys." Open the curiosity gap
                   out loud: tease the thought, never give it away. Usually the NOTICE,
                   said with tension. ("I finished a book last night and I'm a little
                   annoyed at it — in the best way.")

2. EXPLANATION     Get immediately into it. Walk the CONNECT conversationally —
                   the observation unfolding, the worlds linking. One idea only.
                   Concrete beats abstract; story beats over list points.

3. REHOOK (~60–70%) A twist that buys the last third: "but here's the part I didn't
                   see coming…" / "and then I realised the actual problem was…" /
                   "but the bit I can't stop thinking about is…". Save the best —
                   the REFRAME — for after this beat. Never front-load it.

4. CLOSE           The reframe lands → then ONE short line: an open question or a
                   soft CTA ("save this for…", "tell me yours"). Let it hang;
                   don't over-explain the thought you just gave them.

CAPTION:           Short. Restate the reframe in one line + the open question.
```

**Voice guard for reels:** the HOOK promises a *thought*, not "value"; the EXPLANATION is a story unfolding, not steps/numbers/hacks. If the script starts sounding like "3 things you need to…", it's drifted — rewrite curiosity-first.

### Format D — The "Quote it left me with" (Pillar 1)
*Not* a book review. A line from a book/essay + what it made you rethink.

```
[VISUAL] The quote, beautifully set (or you holding the book).
[CAPTION] "I didn't underline this because it's pretty. I underlined it because it ruined a belief I had about [X]."
          → the noticing, the connection, the one new thought. Soft reading-club energy.
```

## Instagram voice notes (different from LinkedIn)
- More sensory, more first-person, more feeling. "I noticed…", "I keep thinking about…", "there's something about…"
- It's okay to be quiet, small, tender. Not every post needs to be clever.
- Playful wit lands well here — but still curiosity-first. A soft smile, not a punchline.
- Never "random vlog." Even a coffee needs a lens — the *thought* it carried.
- Aesthetics matter, but the aesthetic is *in service of the thought*, not a replacement for it.

## Worked examples (in voice — placeholders in [brackets])

### Example 1 — Carousel · Pillars 4 + 3 ("the matcha ritual")

- **S1 (cover):** "I've made my matcha the exact same way for [300] mornings. I think it's the least productive — and most important — thing I do."
- **S2 (notice):** "Same bowl. Same whisk. Same two minutes where nothing else is allowed to happen."
- **S3:** "It's not about the matcha. I don't even always finish it."
- **S4 (connect):** "It's the one part of my day I refuse to optimise. No podcast. No 'while I…'. Just the whisk."
- **S5:** "We've been taught that every spare minute should *do* something. So the un-optimised minutes started to feel like guilt."
- **S6 (reframe):** "But attention isn't a resource I spend. It's the thing my whole life is actually made of. And I'd been renting all of it out."
- **S7:** "The matcha is just where I take two minutes back."
- **S8 (close):** "What's your un-optimisable two minutes? (Save this for a morning you forget you have one.) 🍵"

*Pillar 4 (ritual) + Pillar 3 (attention). Reframe = attention isn't spent, it's what life is made of.*

### Example 2 — Caption-led single · Pillars 4 + 2 (a painting)

> **[Image: your painting, mid-progress, imperfect on purpose]**
>
> I almost didn't post this because the left side is "wrong."
>
> Then I remembered every painting I love has a wrong side. That's usually the part the artist stopped controlling.
>
> Beginner work has a thing the polished stuff loses: you can *see the deciding.* The hesitation. The moment they went "eh, good enough" and it became the best part.
>
> Maybe that's true of more than paintings.
>
> The wrong side is where the person is. 🎨
>
> What did you make recently that you almost hid?

*Pillar 4 (art) + Pillar 2 (creativity). Reframe = imperfection is where the person is. Beginner-friendly, invites the shy makers.*

---

### Example 3 — Talking-head Reel · Pillars 1 + 3

> **Hook (to camera):** "I finished a book last night and I'm a little annoyed at it — in the best way."
>
> **Middle:** "It argued that we don't actually choose what we love. We choose what we *pay attention to*, and love is just what's left standing after enough attention. Which is either the most romantic thing I've read or the most inconvenient."
>
> **Reframe:** "Because it means taste isn't something you *have*. It's something you're building, every time you decide what's worth a second look."
>
> **Caption:** "Currently deciding what's worth a second look. 📖 What are you paying attention to lately — on purpose?"

*Pillar 1 (book) + Pillar 3 (taste/attention). Reframe = taste is built by attention, not innate.*

---

### Example 4 — "Quote it left me with" · Pillar 1

> **[Visual: the line, set cleanly on a calm background]**
> *"[the underlined line]"*
>
> **Caption:** "I didn't underline this because it's beautiful. I underlined it because it quietly dismantled something I believed about [X].
>
> [2–3 lines: what you believed → what the line did to it → the one new thought.]
>
> Adding this to the growing list of sentences that live in my head rent-free. What's living in yours? 🔖"

*Pillar 1. Soft reading-club seed ("growing list", "what's in yours"). Not a review — a rethink.*

## Caption CTA bank (warm, not salesy)
- "Save this for the next time you [X]."
- "What's your version of this?"
- "Tell me I'm not the only one who [X]."
- "What are you paying attention to lately?"
- "Adding to the list — what's on yours?" *(reading-club seed)*
- Sometimes just an emoji and silence. Let the thought breathe.
