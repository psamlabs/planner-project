## MON · LinkedIn · 🧠 Notice-of-the-Week twin · Pillar 4+5
**Café hands you the cup before asking → your onboarding is a café counter.**

**Copy:**

> The café near my flat hands you the cup before they ask "for here, or to go?"
>
> I only noticed because everywhere else asks first — while you're still a stranger, still deciding, still holding your wallet.
>
> This one commits to you first. Cup in your hand, warm, yours. *Then* the question.
>
> It's a tiny sequence. It's also an entire philosophy of trust.
>
> Most brands ask for the commitment before they've given anything. The email before the value. The demo before the "aha." The "quick call" before you've felt understood. We draw it as a funnel. The customer feels it as a toll booth.
>
> The café flipped the order. Give first. Ask second. By the time they ask anything of you, you already feel looked after — so the ask doesn't land as a cost.
>
> Your onboarding is a café counter. The real question isn't "how do we capture them faster." It's "what can we put in their hands before we ask them for anything."
>
> Where in your funnel are you asking before you've given?

**Hashtags:** none, or `#customerexperience` `#brandstrategy`.
**Visual:** text-only, or a warm photo of a coffee cup being handed over.
**Fill before posting:** confirm this is a real café you go to (or swap in the real "give first" moment you noticed).

## WED · LinkedIn · 💼 Stacked build-in-public — THE REVEAL · Pillar 5+1
**⭐ The highest-stakes post of the month — how your audience meets Stacked. Proof-of-work, not a launch. No hard CTA. Link in bio only.**

**Copy:**

> For the last few months I've been quietly building something. Not a client project. My own.
>
> It's a book app. I built it because every reading app I tried kept doing the one thing I didn't want.
>
> They all want to tell you what to read *next.* Feed you a queue. Rank you, recommend to you, optimise your reading like it's a metric.
>
> I didn't want another feed. I wanted a *map.* I wanted to see the shelf I already have — what I own, what I've read, and, more than anything, the gaps. The series missing its middle book. The trilogy I never finished. The author I've read three of and always meant to complete.
>
> So I made **Stacked.** *Your shelf, mapped.* No algorithm telling you what's next — it just shows you your own reading, honestly, holes and all.
>
> Here's the strategist part, and the reason I'm sharing it here: building it taught me something no brief ever did. "No algorithm" isn't a missing feature. It's a *position.* It's me deciding I'd rather you trust your own shelf than outsource your taste to a recommendation engine.
>
> Positioning was never about what you add. It's about what you're willing to refuse.
>
> I'm not selling anything — it's early, it's mine, and it's in my bio if you're the kind of person who'd rather map your shelf than be fed one. Mostly I just wanted to say: the fastest I've ever understood branding was having to brand something I actually care about.
>
> What's a series *you* never finished — and was it the book's fault, or did you just lose the thread?

**Hashtags:** none, or `#buildinpublic` `#brandstrategy`.
**Visual:** ⭐ **attach a clean screenshot of Stacked** — ideally the shelf/map view showing a series gap. One honest screenshot beats any graphic. (If not ready, text-only still works.)
**Notes:** the closing question is genuine engagement *and* quiet user research — the answers are future Stacked feature briefs. Do not add "sign up now" energy; the restraint is the strategy.
