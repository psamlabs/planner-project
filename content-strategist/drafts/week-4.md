**Creativity as a daily practice, not lightning — the strategic twin of Tuesday.**

**Copy:**

> I cook almost every day. Nobody has ever asked me if I was "feeling inspired" to make dinner.
>
> But creative work gets held to the lightning standard. Wait for the idea. Protect the muse. Don't force it.
>
> Five years of watching creative teams (and myself) says the opposite: the people who produce the most original work treat it like cooking. >
> Because creativity isn't the lightning. It's the practice of *noticing* — and noticing is a muscle that only stays warm if you use it on the ordinary days too.
>
> The daily practice isn't the tax you pay to earn the breakthrough. The daily practice is where the breakthrough was built, invisibly, all along.
>
> Inspiration is just competence catching the light.
>
> What's your version of daily cooking — the unglamorous practice your best work secretly comes from?

## WED · LinkedIn · 💼 Stacked build-in-public — the cost of the position · Pillar 5+2
**Beat 4: every real position has a price. Goes deeper than the reveal instead of repeating it.**

**Copy:**

> When I shared Stacked here, the most common question was some version of: "But wouldn't recommendations make it better?"
>
> Honestly? For some people — yes. That's the uncomfortable part of positioning nobody puts on the slide.
>
> "No algorithm" costs me things. It costs the dopamine of a fresh queue. It costs the users who genuinely want to be told what's next. It probably costs growth — recommendation loops are the best retention machine ever built, and I left it out on purpose.
>
> But here's what I've learned, building this and watching brands for five years: a position that costs you nothing isn't a position. It's a slogan.
>
> Every brand says "we're different." Almost none can answer: *what does your difference cost you, and why do you pay it anyway?* That answer is the position. Everything else is font choice.
>
> I pay the no-algorithm price because the entire point of Stacked is that your shelf is *yours* — a map of your own attention, not a feed of someone else's predictions. Add the algorithm and the product still works. It just stops meaning anything.
>
> So, a question I now ask every brand I work with, and myself: what are you refusing — and what does the refusal cost you? If the second answer is "nothing," you haven't positioned. You've decorated.
>
> What does your difference cost you?

**Hashtags:** none, or `#positioning` `#brandstrategy`.
**Visual:** text-only is right for this one.

## FRI · LinkedIn · 🎬 "What I learned about AI this week" · Pillar 2
**Through a storytelling lens, not a tool tutorial.**

**Copy:**

> What I learned about AI this week, as a writer:
>
> I asked it to write about [something you actually tried — e.g. my morning matcha ritual]. The draft came back instantly. Grammatical, warm-ish, shaped like the thing I would write.
>
> And completely empty of the one detail that made the ritual mine — [the real detail — e.g. that I never finish the cup, and that's somehow the point].
>
> Not because the model is bad. Because it wasn't *there.* It can render "a cozy morning ritual" in flawless prose forever. What it can't do is have been standing in my kitchen when I noticed.
>
> Which clarified something for me: AI just made drafting nearly free. And when the draft becomes free, everything that was scarce about writing moves upstream — to the noticing. The specific, unphotogenic, only-you detail is now the entire price of admission.
>
> AI lowered the cost of writing. It raised the price of having something to say.
>
> The writers and brands that struggle next won't be the ones who refuse the tools. They'll be the ones with nothing observed — just opinions about observations other people made.
>
> What's the last thing you noticed that no model could have? That's your moat. Guard it by using it.

**Hashtags:** none, or `#ai` `#contentstrategy`.
**Visual:** text-only.
