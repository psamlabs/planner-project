### 1. The power of knowing what you like — *For The Record* (fortherecordink)
- **Link:** https://open.substack.com/pub/fortherecordink/p/the-power-of-knowing-what-you-like
- **Read on:** 2026-07-14 · **Rating:** 🌟 loved · **Themes:** taste, identity, psychology, creativity
- **Sparked:** 💡 seeded an idea (links to "relearning my hobbies")
- **One-line takeaway:** "Confidence isn't built by being liked. It's built by liking what you like."

**Parts she loved:**
> **Collective wisdom** — Steve Jobs: "Everything around you that you call life was made up by people no smarter than you… you can build your own things that other people can use." We're all borrowing from a shared creative ecosystem. Collecting cues, quirks, and influences that reflect our unique way of seeing.

> You don't even need to know *why* you like what you like. Over time, familiar themes emerge (like handwriting). That becomes your signature.

> **Confidence isn't built by being liked. It's built by liking what you like.** The antidote to comparison and the remedy for indecision. You can't buy taste. You can't fake alignment.

> **The gap** — Self-discrepancy theory (E. Tory Higgins): negative emotions arise from the gap between our *actual* self and our *ideal* / *ought* self. Outsourcing our taste widens the gap; every choice that feels true bridges it a little.

**Her take (verbatim):**
> In a world of people trying to tell you what's right and wrong — because of what *they* think it is — I think it's better to first know what's right for yourself, instead of blindly following others.
>
> Same with this: I'm relearning my hobbies. Just to enjoy them as hobbies. Not as tasks. Not as something to be good at.

**Content potential:** spine for the "relearning my hobbies" idea (taste → the gap → relearning to express, not impress). Possible Rent-Free line: *"Confidence isn't built by being liked. It's built by liking what you like."*
