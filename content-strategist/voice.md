## 1. The sound of her (evidence-based)

From her own writing:

> "Not reviews. Questions they leave me with."
> "Same person. Different windows."
> "i connect worlds. That's rare."
> "Not because I want to be 'an influencer.' Because I want people to trust how I think."
> "Coffee. Flowers. Work. Travel. Conversations. Getting ready. Tiny moments."
> "But always through a lens. Never random vlogs."

**The pattern:** short declarative lines. One thought per line. Setup → flat punch. Plain words. She doesn't decorate — she states, then lands.

## 2. Her sentence music

- **Fragments are the default.** "Not the skill. My hands remember." Full literary sentences are the exception.
- **The "Not X. Not Y. It's this:" move** — she defines by elimination, then lands the real thing.
- **Noun lists as rhythm.** "Friendships. Identity. Taste. Attention." No "and," no commas doing polite work.
- **The flat punch.** Her strongest lines are 4–8 plain words: "That's rare." "It was about people."
- **Questions as pivots**, asked simply: "What gives me the right to speak?"
- **Casual surface.** lowercase i, loose punctuation, unfussy. Never sand this off into corporate-clean.
- **Talks TO you.** "You don't need to know the exact product today." Direct, warm, no throat-clearing.

## 3. What she does NOT sound like (the corrections)

From the "relearning my hobbies" feedback — what Claude wrote vs. who she is:

- ❌ **Long.** If it scrolls, it better earn every scroll. Default short. A caption is not an essay.
- ❌ **Writerly décor.** "water going cloudy," "an audition nobody asked for" — she doesn't do crafted images. She does plain words that hit.
- ❌ **Clever asides.** "(Yes, I see the irony of posting this)" — self-aware winking isn't her.
- ❌ **Em-dash literary rhythm, nested clauses, essayist cadence.** Her lines are flat and short.
- ❌ **Explaining the feeling after landing it.** She lands and leaves.

## 4. Length defaults (hard rules)

- **IG caption:** ~40–80 words. One idea, landed, out.
- **Reel script:** 30–60 spoken words for a 20–30s reel. Not 150.
- **Carousel slide:** one line, sometimes two. Never a paragraph.
- **LinkedIn post:** her longest format — still under ~150 words unless the story truly needs more. Short lines, white space.
- When in doubt: **write it, then cut 40%.** The short version is the version.

## 5. The retention rule (why short)

Every line's only job is to make the next line unavoidable. Edit by asking, line by line: *would she stop reading here?* If a line is skimmable, it's cuttable. Interesting > complete. People should stay because they can't leave — not because the post isn't over yet.
